What these numbers mean

The percentages are estimates, not universal biological constants. They vary with body composition, diet, age, environment and analytical methods. The many decimal places for extremely small quantities reflect conversion arithmetic, not equivalent measurement precision. Published reference datasets can disagree markedly for trace elements.

The remaining 58 elements have no generally accepted fixed percentage in a normal human body. Some may be present through environmental exposure, while certain synthetic or short-lived elements are not normally present.

Biological importance is not proportional to abundance: iodine, selenium, cobalt and molybdenum are essential in tiny amounts, while lead, cadmium and mercury can be present without any established beneficial function.
